YOU DON’T NEED TO KNOW EVERYTHING

Just take the first step.

A little planning helps you spend less time worrying and more time looking around.

01 / CHOOSE WELL

Keep your first outing small.

Pick a short, clearly marked route with modest elevation gain. Check the official land manager’s trail description, closures, access rules, and facilities. Allow time for breaks and a comfortable return before dark.

02 / GO TOGETHER

Bring good company.

Go with someone you trust and stay together at the pace of the slowest person. Young hikers should plan with a parent or trusted adult and have appropriate adult supervision. Tell someone your route and return time.

03 / PACK SMART

A few essentials matter.

Bring enough water for everyone, extra food, sun protection, layers, a rain layer, a headlamp, a small first-aid kit, a whistle, and an offline map. Wear comfortable shoes with grip. Don’t rely on cell service or water being available on the trail.

04 / WATCH THE SKY

Utah conditions change.

Check the forecast and official alerts. Avoid the hottest part of the day; carry extra water and turn back before running low. Avoid exposed ridges during thunderstorms. Never enter a slot canyon when flash flooding is possible, including from distant storms. Snow and ice require different skills and equipment.

05 / LEAVE IT LOVELY

Be a thoughtful visitor.

Stay on designated trails, pack out all trash, give wildlife space, and leave plants and rocks where they are. Respect other visitors, cultural sites, posted rules, and tribal lands. Check pet restrictions before bringing a dog.

06 / LISTEN TO YOURSELF

Turning back is a good plan.

Take breaks and speak up if you feel tired, overheated, cold, or uncomfortable. Reaching a destination is optional; getting home safely is the goal. In an emergency, call 911 if you can connect. A phone may have no coverage.
