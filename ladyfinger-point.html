Why this hike is worthwhile

Meet a different side of Utah: island scenery, open skies, and a short route toward Ladyfinger Point. The small distance makes room to slow down, notice the Great Salt Lake landscape, and enjoy being outdoors together. Keep wildlife encounters at a respectful distance and let the views be the reward.

Know Before You Go

Preparation

Bring water, sun protection, and layers for changing conditions. Check the official map before leaving your vehicle. Allow extra time for views, wildlife, and any trail conditions that slow your group.

Seasonal hazards

Give free-ranging bison and other wildlife ample space; never approach or disturb them. Biting midges can be a problem in spring (the park identifies April–June); ordinary repellent is ineffective against these gnats, and fine-mesh head nets are recommended. Mosquitoes can also be present in warmer months.

Seasons and weather

Editorial beginner planning seasons: spring and fall. The park describes non-motorized recreation year-round, but that does not guarantee a clear, comfortable, or open trail. Spring insects can be intense; check the conditions page. Winter and summer demand their own weather assessment.

Plan for exposed, changeable conditions: bring sun protection and a layer for wind or cold. Check the forecast and avoid heat, storms, snow, or ice that exceed your group’s abilities.

Fees

The official fee schedule lists $15 per standard vehicle (up to eight people). State annual-pass users should check the separate Davis County causeway-fee rules on the same page. Recheck rates before travel.

Access and restrictions

Backcountry access is restricted to designated trails; no cross-country travel. Dogs must remain leashed. Check park hours, special-event restrictions, and closures before visiting; rideshare pickup is not available according to the park.
