Why this hike is worthwhile

A small adventure with a dramatic reveal: a sandy approach leads to a sculpted slot in Snow Canyon’s sandstone cliffs. Look closely at the wind-carved pockets and textures, or take the signed option toward the overlook if it suits your group. There is plenty to notice without needing a long day on the trail.

Know Before You Go

Preparation

Wear shoes that grip uneven rock and are comfortable in deep sand. Bring water and sun protection even for this short outing. Follow the marked trail across the dry wash and choose the slot or overlook branch according to your group’s comfort.

Seasonal hazards

Deep sand and uneven rock can slow a group. A short canyon is still a place to avoid during storms or when flooding is possible; check official weather and park advice before entering. Extreme desert heat can overwhelm young hikers quickly.

Seasons and weather

Editorial beginner planning seasons: spring and fall, based on the park’s extreme-summer-heat warning. These are not guarantees of mild weather or canyon access. Confirm seasonal restrictions with the park; do not confuse this route with Johnson Canyon’s separate seasonal closure.

The park warns of very hot summer conditions and advises avoiding activity between 11 a.m. and 7 p.m. in summer. Take heat advisories seriously, carry water, and skip the outing if conditions do not suit your group. Do not enter the slot during threatening rain or storms.

Fees

State-park day-use fee: the reviewed schedule lists $15 per vehicle for Utah residents and $20 for non-residents (up to eight people). Check the official fee page for your group and current rates.

Access and restrictions

Use designated, mapped trails only; off-trail travel harms fragile habitat. Check park hours and road/event closures. The park currently publishes a six-foot maximum dog-leash rule; consult current pet rules before bringing a dog.
