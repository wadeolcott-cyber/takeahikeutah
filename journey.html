A LIFETIME OF SMALL STEPS

Your Hiking Journey

Grow in confidence, judgment, and care for the outdoors. Start where you are and revisit the basics whenever you need.

Stages are a guide to learning, not certifications or fixed difficulty ratings. Choose routes for your whole group, current conditions, and relevant skills. Mentoring means supporting others’ choices, not pushing their limits.

STAGE 01

First Steps

Start with short, approachable outings and learn the essentials.

Choose a manageable route, pack the basics, stay with your group, and get comfortable turning back.

PLAN FOR THE DAY YOU GO

Every season changes the journey.

A seasonal label is a planning starting point, never a report of current conditions. Check official forecasts, closures, and land-manager guidance before every trip.

Spring

Changing weather, muddy trails, and lingering snow can affect access. Check conditions at your route’s elevation; lower ground is not automatically clear or safe.

Browse spring guides →

Summer

Plan around heat and thunderstorms. Higher elevations may still hold snow. Check forecasts and water needs, and have a conservative turnaround plan.

Browse summer guides →

Fall

Shorter days and colder nights change your margin for error. Pack layers and lighting, and check for early snow and access restrictions.

Browse fall guides →

Winter

Snow, ice, and avalanche terrain require specialized assessment and skills. A summer trail rating does not describe winter difficulty. Seek appropriate training and official forecasts.

Browse winter guides →
